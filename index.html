I build Thai-language AI at iApp, where I lead the AI and R&D departments and still write code most days. My team's models include OpenThai Legal, OpenThaiGPT, ChindaLLM, and ChindaMT; together with our speech and vision systems, they have served more than 70 million API calls. I work across the whole stack, from post-training with SFT, DPO, and GRPO down to vLLM and TensorRT-LLM serving on our own GPUs.

I hold a doctorate from Institute of Science Tokyo (formerly Tokyo Institute of Technology), where I worked on character-based word segmentation in the Okumura–Funakoshi Lab. These days I also advise the Big Data Institute on inference serving for ThaiLLM, Thailand's sovereign language model programme.

Some of this is easy to check: Tech Collective named iApp a top-five AI startup in Thailand in 2026, our face anti-spoofing holds an iBeta Level 1 certification, and the models and papers are on open.iapp.co.th and arXiv.

Work I'd point you to first

OpenThai 2.0 Legal (2026) — an open-weight Thai legal language model, built with NVIDIA, BDI, and AIEAT on Nemotron-3-Nano-30B. Legal Thai is formal, precise, and badly served by general models; this one is free to download and run.

OpenThaiGPT 1.6 & R1 (2025) — Thai-centric reasoning models. R1 is built on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-32B and beats DeepSeek-R1-70B on Thai reasoning benchmarks at half the size: 56.7 against 33.3 on AIME24-TH. There is a paper, and the weights are public: 1.6-72B, R1-32B.

CIBbot (2026) — a legal-advice and incident-reporting assistant we built for the Royal Thai Police's Central Investigation Bureau. It runs fully air-gapped over seven bureau archives and answers with 96.6% retrieval accuracy on a 208-item legal evaluation. It was launched at CIB headquarters with the National Research Council, and you can try it.

one.iapp.co.th and open.iapp.co.th (2026) — iApp's two public faces. One sells our text, voice, and vision APIs; open gives models away under Apache-2.0, including Chinda LLM 4B, ChindaMT, and the ChindaTTS voices.

Certified biometric liveness (2024) — dual-stage face anti-spoofing that passed iBeta Level 1 Presentation Attack Detection testing with a 0% attack presentation classification error rate. We have since extended it to passive deepfake screening for banking.

ThaiLLM (2025–) — I advise the Big Data Institute on serving infrastructure for the national 8B and 30B models, drawing on our own vLLM and TensorRT-LLM benchmarks on H100.
